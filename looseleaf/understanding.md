The interpreter has the obligation to render the message without changing, adding, subtracting, or omitting. 

You might think that the interpreter has the obligation to *ensure understanding*.

Alas; does *anyone* fully understand *everything* that happens in this system? 

No. No one does.

You'd think the judges understand, and you could be forgiven for laboring under that illusion. Every judge is on their own journey of learning the law, the procedure, the regulations -- and all of those change, often rapidly and without warning. And the law that is applicable in a case may not be the law that is applicable *in this moment*. Nor do they know all the ins and outs of what their own administrative and support staff does. Do the judges understand everything that the interpreters in every language have to deal with as they make their split-second judgment calls?

Understanding is always partial. Understanding is always evolving; it is always subject to the economies of reality.
